# The Eldritch Bestiary — boss riff night, 2026-07-22

Khaled's thesis: **unimaginable eldritch threats don't have to be 'horror.'**
They are eldritch. No slenderman aesthetics required — in fact Slenderman
himself is just a boss you fight ("Go fight slenderman, slayer. Figure it
out."), which is the joke that proves the rule: horror AESTHETICS aren't
eldritch. True eldritch operates on alien logic.

Kore's structural read (confirmed by every entry): each of these bosses
violates a different LAW OF THE MEDIUM — the game's own ontology — not the
player's nerves. That's what eldritch means in a game:

| Boss | The rule of games it breaks |
|---|---|
| It Eats Color | "the world stays rendered" (attacks the adjective compiler) |
| Innocence | "enemies acknowledge you" (no aggro table exists for her) |
| Arbelos | "bodies are 3D flesh" (2D sacred geometry in a 3D world) |
| Water Elemental | "damage reduces health" (health bar is VOLUME; weapon is a bucket) |
| The Lawyers | "your tools are yours" (they seal your kit, legally, with due process) |
| Slenderman | "the horror icon is special" (he's just a guy; figure it out) |
| Quelaag Den | (the thesis-in-miniature freebie: triumph → snap → purple rain) |

## Innocence

An imperious, unblinking, horizon-staring woman. Might carry arms, might just
wear robes. She does not look at you, or anyone at all. Her eyes just look
forward. Her posture is immaculate — not because she's trying, but because
there isn't an urge to lean or slouch or favor one side. "She just stands
straight. The way a column is vertical. The way a knife is sharp. She is not
coy. She is not angry. She is never sarcastic. She stares at something that no
matter how many times you blink or rub your eyes you cannot see."

Throw tomatoes at the statue — unblemished. Swing a hammer as hard as you can —
no crack. Pound and pound, scream and yell — she never even looks at you.

(Open design space: what does "fighting" her even mean? Candidate reads:
the fight is EARNING acknowledgment; or she is never fightable and the payoff
is floor-9+ sight finally revealing what she stares at — possibly rendered
outside the compiler's vocabulary, tying her to the divinity theology.)

## Arbelos

Corrupted angels locked in the dungeon. They are circles and radii and sectors
and diameters and apothems. Attack: extending a hand — a 2D circle stretching
into an extreme oblong, gomu-gomu-pistol but it's just a circle becoming an
oval. "Wheres your holy water. His distorted synth cries."

(Name is the geometry deep-cut — the shoemaker's-knife figure. These are the
jumbled-polygon divinity theology weaponized: sacred geometry, fallen. Engine-
native: flat primitives in 3D space, the tween IS the attack animation. The
low-poly aesthetic isn't a budget constraint here — it's the horror itself.)

## The Water Elemental

Seriously fight a water elemental. Sword-slice an arm off at mach speed — it
reconstitutes. Fire? Lol. Air? Nope. Lightning? Congratulations, you've
electrified the water; enjoy getting buzzed when it hits you.

But scoop a bucket of it out and throw it somewhere else — THAT PART'S GONE.

"Better pick up a bucket and get to bucketing, slayer."

(Health bar = volume. Weapon = bucket. The dungeon's answer to a god of water
is manual labor — the most blue-collar boss fight ever designed. The janitor
fantasy at the bottom of the world.)

## The Lawyers

Six supernatural lawyers (Pact energy). That's the boss fight. Six lawyers.
They constantly SEAL your tools. Use a restricted tool → they warn you, as
obliged by supernatural law. Try it again → enjoy getting smited.

"Just a fistfight with six lawyers. That's the only legal way to do it."

(The anti-power-fantasy boss and the extraction theme incarnate: they take
take take, legally, with warnings and due process. The fight is subtraction —
sword sealed, spells sealed, knives sealed — until the only unrestricted tool
you own is your fists. Your build stripped to your body.)
